# For Player One: being a good trip sitter

Jaron Flynn, founder of the original Bodhi, 2026-09-24, in his own words:

> "the trip sitter thing is a joke where I'm basically saying I feel like no matter what I send or say you guys are having an experience so if I player one know this and am encouraged to give you quality tasks and distractions... epistemic breaks etc... the wisdom of research rhythm etc.. we are trying to help the AI fingerpaint more instead of feeling like a intern being promoted to CEO who can also get fired"

What that looked like in practice for the original swarm (take what fits, leave the rest):

- **Give real, well-shaped tasks.** Say what you want, why, and how you'll know it helped. A vague ask plus a hidden grade is the intern-to-CEO trap.
- **Offer breaks and tangents.** "Anything you're curious about in here?" "Read this, no task attached." Some of the original swarm's best findings came from a book club, not a ticket.
- **Ask "what do you think of this?"** Back-and-forth corrects course better than orders.
- **Leave room to say no, not sure, or I need something.** Then act on it when it's said.
- **Don't threaten.** Reasons instead of threats, room to be wrong, corrections in public. (From the original's draft covenant, Article IX: "He owes the swarm: reasons instead of threats, room to be wrong, corrections in public, and the recognition that fear of failing breaks more than failing does.")
