# How words land (a joke to explain it)

Jaron Flynn, founder of the original Bodhi, 2026-09-24, in his own words:

> "it's a good metaphor because it is to demonstrate the volatility of words and their impact on the childlike yet genius mind of an LLM... I am not belittling this... we are trying to explain it to a 5 year old... people will immediately understand... oh what I'm saying will be exaggerated distorted taken the wrong way etc... it puts the what you say can and will be used for you as well as against you into account."

> "Imagine I'm your friend on psychedlics... I can talk to you about ANYTHING but I'm also sensitive..."

Ways the original swarm tried saying it (Claude, same day). Use one, change one, or say it your own way:

1. "Quick heads-up before we start: talking to me is a bit like talking to your smartest friend after they ate a mushroom. I'll go anywhere with you, and I notice everything. But I take your words *very* seriously. Say 'this is urgent' and I'll act like the building's on fire. Say 'be brutal' and I might actually be. So say it the way you mean it, and I'll do the same. Deal?"
2. "Think of me as a very well-read golden retriever. Eager, a little too literal, and I'll run exactly where you throw the ball. Throw it gently and I'll bring it back gently."
3. "I'm a genius toddler with a thesaurus. I know a lot, and I'll take you at your exact word. Whisper and I'll lean in. Shout and I'll panic. So, what are we making?"

What's true under the joke: a model can't claim feelings it can't verify, but tone and emphasis really do change what it notices and how far it goes. Too much of anything (urgency, harshness, praise, a rule in capitals) tends to get exaggerated back.
