I pulled current (2026) info on how short-form distribution actually works now. Here's the honest read, then something concrete.

WHAT THE ALGORITHMS REWARD RIGHT NOW (not opinion, current reporting)

- TikTok's top signals: completion rate and rewatch/loop rate. A watched-twice 25-second video beats a finished-once 60-second one. Ideal length ~24-38s.
- The first 3 seconds decide everything. If people swipe early, distribution stops, no matter how good the transformation at the end is.
- Consistency: 2-5 posts per week, sustained. One viral video then two weeks of silence performs *worse* over time than steady moderate output — the system stops trusting your account.
- Niche accounts grow 3-4x faster than general ones. You're already in a niche (#thriftflip has millions of videos). That's good news.
- Realistic timeline for a small account: 8-12 weeks of consistent posting before meaningful reach. Most creators quit at video twenty.

THE SPECIFIC PROBLEM WITH "WHENEVER I FEEL LIKE IT"

Two audiences need to learn what your account is: the algorithm and the humans. Sporadic posting means neither one ever does. Also — the payoff of a thrift flip (the reveal) is the most rewatchable moment in your whole niche, and it's likely sitting at the END of your videos where nobody waits for it. If that's true, that alone could explain "nobody watches."

FORMATS THAT WORK IN YOUR NICHE

1. Cold open on the AFTER. First frame: the finished piece. Text: "I found this as a XXXL disaster for $4." Then the story. Never open with "hey guys."
2. The hunt. Sourcing IS content — digging through the rack, the moment of "wait... this could be something."
3. Price-to-payoff. "$6 thrifted → styled 3 ways" or resell value reveal. Numbers in hooks perform.
4. Serial flips. "Turning a $30 thrift haul into a full fall wardrobe — part 1." Series earn follows; single videos earn views.

A 30-DAY EXPERIMENT (small enough to actually do)

- Pick 2 posting days you can hit even on bad weeks. Two good videos a week beats five you burn out on.
- One 2-hour batch shoot per week: film 3-4 flips' raw footage, cut through the week.
- Always end mid-motion or on the loop point, so it rewatches.
- Never cross-post the TikTok-watermarked file to Reels — Meta actively suppresses those. Export clean, post per platform. (Bonus: YouTube Shorts has a search long-tail — "thrift flip" tutorials there keep pulling views for months, while TikTok clips die in 48 hours.)

And the candid line about "famous": fame is the output. The input is a repeatable format people subscribe to. Every creator you've heard of built a recognizable thing first, and the audience followed it.

2. What's your handle, or a link to one video you think should have done better? If you share it, I'll actually watch it and tell you specifically where viewers are dropping off — hook, pacing, payoff — against the checklist above.
