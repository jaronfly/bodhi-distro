TWO PROBLEMS, AND ONLY ONE OF THEM IS YOUR POSTING

1. "Whenever I feel like it" is the killer. Every post is a test where the algorithm learns who your audience is; sporadic posting keeps resetting that lesson, and no audience habit forms. The consensus for short-form in 2026: the minimum effective cadence is about 4-5 posts a week, held for months. The compounding is back-loaded — months 4-6 usually outperform months 1-3 combined, which is exactly when most people quit.

2. But frequency alone won't fix views-per-video. What stops the scroll is the first 2 seconds. The single most reliable thrift-flip opener is the price spread: "I paid $4 for this. It sells for $80." Lead with the number, the brand, or the ugliness of the before — never a slow intro. If viewers bounce before your reveal, the video never gets distributed no matter how good the transformation is.

WHAT'S WORKING IN YOUR NICHE RIGHT NOW

- Price-spread cold opens (resale content lives and dies on this)
- Before/after transformation montages set to trending audio, under 30 seconds
- "Thinking out loud" thrifting — filming the decisions, showing yourself picking up a blazer, examining it, putting it back, saying why. One creator pivoted to this and her average views went from 180K to 290K in eight weeks
- How-to content (repair techniques, styling) gets shared about 30% more than simple hauls
- Comments as content: respond to everything; every question in your comments is the next video's brief

A CAUTION ABOUT "FAMOUS"

The viral cases I found (like the 50M-view curtain dress) were followed by weeks of deliberate work — follow-ups, comment-driven sequels, platform-specific repurposing. The video is the spark; the system is the career. So we build the system, and let fame be a possible outcome of it.
